## Overview

Hanako-san (花子さん, 'Miss Hanako'), commonly called Toire no Hanako-san ('Hanako of the Toilet'), is a Japanese yokai/yurei tied to school bathrooms. She is described as the spirit of a young girl with a bobbed haircut and a red skirt or dress who haunts a particular stall — usually the third stall of the third-floor girls' restroom — and can be summoned by knocking three times on the door and asking 'Are you there, Hanako-san?' If she answers, consequences in different versions range from a bloody hand pulling the questioner into the toilet, to being dragged to Hell, to encountering a three-headed lizard. Folklorists trace the schoolyard legend to roughly the 1950s; it became a national cultural fixture during the Japanese kokkuri/yokai boom of the late 1980s and the school-horror media wave of the 1990s. There is no canonical origin story: variants attribute her death to a WWII air raid during a game of hide-and-seek, to murder by a parent or stranger, or to bullying-driven suicide. The legend is closely associated with children's storytelling games and 'school seven mysteries' (gakkō no nana fushigi) traditions.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Bound spirit (jibakurei)** _(Traditional Japanese folk belief)_

Within the legend's own logic, Hanako-san is a jibakurei — a spirit bound to the location of her death (a school toilet) and unable to leave it.

### Skeptical

**Modern urban legend / folkloric construct** _(Michael Dylan Foster, Matthew Meyer, Komatsu Kazuhiko)_

Folklorists treat Hanako-san as a modern urban legend whose form was shaped by postwar Japanese schoolyard culture, the kokkuri/yokai boom of the 1980s, and subsequent mass-media adaptation. No claim of literal existence is made by scholarly sources.

**Cautionary children's narrative** _(Folklore studies (Japan))_

The legend functions as a self-imposed test of courage and a way of marking certain school spaces as 'liminal' or off-limits, paralleling the social functions of other school-bound ghost stories.
